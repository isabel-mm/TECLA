A corpus is a collection of authentic texts gathered according to explicit criteria in order to study a language or a language variety. Corpus design begins with a decision about what the corpus is meant to represent. A general corpus aims to cover a language as a whole, whereas a specialised corpus is restricted to a domain, a genre or a group of speakers. Representativeness is the extent to which a sample includes the full range of variability of the population it stands for. A balanced corpus distributes its texts across text types in proportions that reflect the design criteria. Corpus size is measured in tokens, and the number of different word forms is the number of types. The type-token ratio relates both figures, although it depends heavily on corpus size. A reference corpus is a large general corpus used as a point of comparison. When a specialised corpus is compared with a reference corpus, the words that are unusually frequent in the specialised corpus are its keywords. A monitor corpus is updated regularly so that it can track changes in the language. A parallel corpus contains source texts and their translations, while a comparable corpus contains original texts in two or more languages that share the same design criteria.
