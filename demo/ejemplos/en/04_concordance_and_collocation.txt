A concordance displays every occurrence of a search word together with its context. Each occurrence is shown as a concordance line, and the usual layout is the keyword in context format, in which the node word is aligned in the centre of the concordance line. A concordancer is the software tool that produces a concordance. Sorting the concordance lines by the words to the left or to the right of the node word reveals recurrent patterns.

Collocation is the tendency of two words to occur together more often than expected by chance. The node word is the word under study, and a collocate is a word that occurs within the collocation window, which usually spans four or five tokens on each side of the node word. An association measure quantifies the strength of a collocation. Mutual information favours rare collocates, whereas the log-likelihood ratio and the t-score favour frequent collocates, so the choice of association measure changes the list of collocates.

Colligation is the co-occurrence of a word with a grammatical category, and semantic prosody is the tendency of a word to occur with collocates that share a positive or a negative meaning. A lexical bundle is a recurrent sequence of three or more word forms, such as an n-gram that is frequent across many texts.

A word sketch summarises the collocates of a word according to the grammatical relations in which they occur. Concordance lines, collocates and word sketches are complementary: the association measure identifies candidates, and the concordance shows how they are used.
