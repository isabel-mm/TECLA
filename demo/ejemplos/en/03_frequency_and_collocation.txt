Frequency is the most basic statistic in corpus linguistics. A frequency list ranks the word forms or lemmas of a corpus by their number of occurrences. Because corpora differ in size, raw frequency is usually converted into normalised frequency, expressed per million tokens. Word frequency follows a very skewed distribution: a few words are extremely frequent, while a large proportion of the types occur only once. A word that occurs only once in a corpus is a hapax legomenon. Dispersion describes how evenly a word is distributed across the texts of a corpus, and a dispersion measure complements frequency because a word may be frequent only in a single text. A concordance displays every occurrence of a search word together with its context, usually in the keyword in context format. Collocation is the tendency of two words to occur together more often than expected by chance. An association measure, such as mutual information or the log-likelihood ratio, quantifies the strength of a collocation. Keyness compares the frequency of a word in a corpus with its frequency in a reference corpus, and a keyword analysis identifies the words that characterise a corpus.
