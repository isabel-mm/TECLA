Corpus annotation adds interpretative linguistic information to the texts of a corpus. The most common type of annotation is part-of-speech tagging, in which each token receives a tag that indicates its word class. A tagset is the inventory of tags used by a tagger. Lemmatisation groups the inflected word forms of a word under a single lemma, so that the frequency of the lemma can be counted. Syntactic annotation, or parsing, assigns a structure to each sentence, and a corpus annotated in this way is called a treebank. Semantic annotation labels word senses or semantic fields, and named entity recognition identifies the names of people, organisations and places. Manual annotation requires annotation guidelines, which describe each category and the criteria for difficult cases. When two annotators label the same texts, inter-annotator agreement measures how consistently the guidelines can be applied. Automatic annotation is faster, but its error rate must be estimated against a manually annotated sample, often called a gold standard. Annotation tools store the labels either inline, inside the text, or as stand-off annotation, in a separate file that points to positions in the text.
